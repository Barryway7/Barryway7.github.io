abstract: Stroke is a leading cause of death and long-term disability worldwide, and early risk prediction can enable timely intervention. This work performs exploratory data analysis on a clinical stroke dataset and benchmarks machine learning models — Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, Random Forest, and XGBoost — for stroke prediction. Random Forest achieved the best performance with 94.14% accuracy, an F1 score of 0.9697, recall of 0.9880, and precision of 0.9520, while Logistic Regression and Decision Tree reached accuracies of 72.86% and 72.14%, respectively. XGBoost attained 84.85% accuracy but suffered from low precision (0.1813) on the imbalanced data, highlighting the importance of metric selection beyond accuracy for clinical prediction tasks.
